Review a change to lean-http, an HTTP/1.1 client library for Lean 4 (v4.34.1).  Read only: do not edit, build or run anything.  Report defects, with file:line, a confidence (high / medium / low) and the concrete scenario that triggers each.  Guilty until proven innocent: assume there are bugs.

The change wires a pure per-call planner (`LeanHttp/Call.lean`: `Call.start`, `Call.plan`, proved in `LeanHttp/Call/Proofs.lean`, not under review) into the client shell, and adds a streaming API.  Files under review (the whole tree is there for context):

- `LeanHttp/Client.lean`: `CallSpec.loop` (the driver of the planner), `Attempt.run` / `acquire` / `connect` (the pool and the re-dispatch of an exchange whose pooled connection turned out closed), `run`, `withResponse`, `request`.
- `LeanHttp/Body.lean` (new): `Live` (an exchange in progress on a socket: drives the pure exchange core `LeanHttp/Exchange.lean`), `BodyStream` (the body the caller pulls inside the callback of `withResponse`).
- `LeanHttp/Call/Trace.lean` (new): `Call.Event` and `Call.validate`, which replays a record of what the shell did through the planner and checks the shell did what the planner said.
- `LeanHttp/Connect.lean` (now one round of connect attempts per call; the planner owns retries and backoff), `LeanHttp/Config.lean`, `LeanHttp/Error.lean`.
- Tests: `Tests/Net.lean` (driver), `tests/net/run.sh` (real-socket scenarios), `tests/net/evil.py`, `Tests/Unit.lean`.

The diff against the base is `wire.diff` (it does not show the two new files, read them in `tree/`).  `tree/DESIGN.md` and `tree/NOTES.md` give the design and the decisions so far (they are not yet updated for this change).

Things the shell must guarantee; try to break each:

1. The shell is a thin driver of the planner: each request it sends is the planner's `State.current` (with the body its `HopBody` says), it reports how each exchange ended truthfully (the final response head before its body is read, or the exchange's `Failure`), it waits a retry's delay from the time the planner decided and never past the deadline, and it delivers or fails exactly as the step says.  `Call.validate` should reject any trace where it does not; can it be fooled (a shell bug it would accept)?
2. A response that is followed or retried has its body drained within the drain limit (connection kept) or its connection dropped, before the next request.
3. Every connection a call takes from the pool is given back or dropped exactly once, on every path (failures, exceptions, the 408 re-dispatch, the callback raising, the stream used after the callback or from another task).
4. No receive registration (a pending `recv?`) outlives the callback of `withResponse`, nor a call of `request`: on v4.34.1 a pending receive can keep the process from exiting.
5. Streaming: each `read` gives the exchange core credit; what the shell holds never exceeds one read; leaving the callback normally or by an exception abandons the rest (drained or dropped, never pooled unless the core says it is reusable); using the stream after the callback raises an error.  An error the stream raised that leaves the callback becomes the call's `Error`; any other exception is raised again.
6. `Error.delivery` must never say `notSent` when any request of the call may have reached a server (retries, redirects, re-dispatch).
7. A POST (or any request that is not idempotent with a replayable body) is never sent again once a byte of it may have reached the server, except after a 408 on a reused connection (which says the server did not process it).
8. Values carried across an asynchronous wait are never updated in place by the runtime (they are shared between threads), so nothing may grow across waits by appending (`NOTES.md`, "Nothing carried across a wait is updated in place").

Also look for: races between the callback ending and a read in another task; anything that can make a call hang or exceed its deadline; misleading docstrings; dead code; complexity that buys nothing.  Keep every informed concern, with its confidence.

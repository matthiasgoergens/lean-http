#### 1. [High] `Cancel.watch` delivers token cancellation via `Selectable.combine` (`IO.bindTask`, not inline), breaking pre-cancelled tokens, post-`shutdown` calls, and same-thread cancellation checks
- **Where**: [`Cancel.watch`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Cancel.lean#L60-L71), [`Cancel.reason?`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Cancel.lean#L84-L86), [`Cancel.onCancel`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Cancel.lean#L88-L95), [`Client.shutdown`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L340-L342), [`CallSpec.loop`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L939-L956), [`Client.run`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L1084-L1089)
- **What goes wrong and how we know**:
  - [`Cancel.watch`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Cancel.lean#L61-L71) claims in its docstring: *"Starts watching `tokens` for a call. A token cancelled already resolves the signal before this returns."* Similarly, [`Cancel.onCancel`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Cancel.lean#L89-L90) claims: *"Runs `f` with the reason once the call is cancelled, in the thread that cancels it"*.
  - Both claims are false. `Cancel.watch` builds each branch with `Selectable.case token.selector fun _ => do signal.resolve (some ((← token.getCancellationReason).getD .cancel))` and runs `discard <| start (Selectable.one ...)`. In `Std.Async.Select`, `Selectable.one` registers every selector and `Selectable.combine` delivers the winner to the `Selectable.case` callback through `IO.bindTask` (**not inline**).
  - Therefore, even when a token in `tokens` is **already cancelled** before `Cancel.watch` is called, `start (Selectable.one ...)` only schedules the `Selectable.case` continuation (`signal.resolve ...`) on the thread pool via `IO.bindTask` and returns immediately with `signal` **still unresolved**.
  - **Consequence A (Calls after `Client.shutdown` fail with `Cause.localClose` instead of `Cause.cancelled .shutdown`)**:
    1. [`Client.shutdown`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L340-L342) cancels `client.closing` with `.shutdown` and calls `client.close` (which marks `s.pool.closed = true`).
    2. A subsequent call enters [`Client.run`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L1084), calls `Cancel.watch (#[client.closing] ++ cancel.toArray)`, and immediately enters [`CallSpec.loop`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L939) on the same thread.
    3. Until a worker thread runs `Selectable.combine`'s `IO.bindTask` task, `spec.cancel.reason?` at line 939 sees `IO.hasFinished c.signal.result? == false` and returns `none`.
    4. `CallSpec.loop` records `Event.send` (line 970) and calls `attempt.run` $\to$ [`Attempt.acquire`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L739-L740) $\to$ `takeConn` (or checks `s.pool.closed` at line 793 if `pool = false`), which synchronously sees the closed pool and returns `.error (notSent .localClose "the client was closed")`.
    5. The call fails with `Cause.localClose` instead of `Cause.cancelled .shutdown`, violating [`Client.shutdown`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L338-L340), [DESIGN.md](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/DESIGN.md#L30), [README.md](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/README.md#L144), and [NOTES.md](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/NOTES.md#L332).
  - **Consequence B (Pre-cancelled caller token sends a request / opens a connection)**:
    - When `cancel` is already cancelled before the call starts, `spec.cancel.reason?` at [`LeanHttp/Client.lean:939`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L939) can return `none` before the worker thread runs `IO.bindTask`. The call then emits `Event.send`, acquires or opens a connection, and—if an idle connection is taken (`idleCheck = false`) before the worker runs—passes line 804 (`a.cancel.reason?`) and starts [`Live.begin`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Body.lean#L378), writing the request to the socket.
  - **Consequence C (Cancelling inside `withResponse` callback and immediately returning pools an unread connection)**:
    - Because `token.cancel` also goes through `Selectable.combine`'s `IO.bindTask` before resolving `c.signal`, if the callback of `withResponse` calls `token.cancel` and immediately returns on the same thread before a worker runs the `IO.bindTask` continuation, [`Live.abandon`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Body.lean#L410) sees `live.cancel.reason? == none`. If the unread response body had already arrived in `live.ex.held` (`live.ex.parser.complete = true`), line 418 steps `.wantBody (2 ^ 62)` synchronously, finishes with `reusable = true`, and **returns the connection to the pool**.
  - **How to fix**: In `Cancel.watch`, check `for token in tokens do if ← token.isCancelled then ...` synchronously before starting `Selectable.one`, and resolve `signal` inline inside a custom `Selector`'s `tryFn` and `registerFn` (`waiter.race` win callback) rather than inside `Selectable.case`'s `IO.bindTask` handler.

#### 3. [High] `BodyStream.read` does not check for cancellation when `pending` is non-empty on a live exchange; and `Client.withResponse` docstring misstates when a full body is unaffected

- **What goes wrong and how we know**:

#### 4. [Medium] Unbounded continuation growth per wait on `Cancel.signal.result?` and per cancelled/timed-out call on `PoolState.freed.result?`
- **Where**: [`Cancel.onCancel`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Cancel.lean#L92-L95), [`Cancel.race`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Cancel.lean#L123-L138), [`Live.begin`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Body.lean#L384-L386), [`PoolState.freed`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L213), [`Attempt.acquire`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L741-L753)
- **What goes wrong and how we know**:
  - **Per-wait growth on `c.signal.result?`**: Throughout an uncancelled call, `c.signal` is an unresolved `IO.Promise`. Every invocation of [`Cancel.race`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Cancel.lean#L130) calls `c.onCancel`, which calls `BaseIO.chainTask (sync := true) c.signal.result? ...`, attaching a new continuation closure (retaining that race's `winner` promise) to `c.signal.result?`. Likewise, every exchange in the call (retries, redirects, redispatches) calls `cancel.onCancel` in [`Live.begin`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Body.lean#L384-L386), attaching a closure retaining that exchange's `mailbox` to `c.signal.result?`. None of these continuations are freed until `watch.finish` runs at the end of the whole call. In particular, when a call waits at `maxActivePerOrigin` ([`LeanHttp/Client.lean:746`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L746)), because `PoolState.freed` is shared across **all** origins ([`LeanHttp/Client.lean:210-213`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Client.lean#L210-L213)), every connection return or drop on *any* origin wakes the waiting call, which loops in `Attempt.acquire` and calls `a.cancel.race` again, adding another continuation to `c.signal.result?` on every wakeup.
  - **Per-call growth on `PoolState.freed.result?`**: Conversely, when all `maxActivePerOrigin` connections are held by long-lived streams so no connection is returned or dropped for an extended time, `PoolState.freed` remains the same unresolved `IO.Promise`. Every call that waits at `.atCap freed` and is cancelled (or times out) leaves its `BaseIO.chainTask (sync := true) task fun r => winner.resolve (.done r)` continuation ([`LeanHttp/Cancel.lean:128`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-notes/cancel/review1/tree/LeanHttp/Cancel.lean#L128)) chained on `freed.result?` until an active connection finally ends.

### Review of the Design Decisions

1. **A new `Cause.cancelled` (with `Std.CancellationReason`) vs reusing `Cause.localClose`**:
   - **Verdict**: Sound choice. `localClose` indicates the client's pool was closed before a connection could be taken (always `notSent`), whereas cancellation can occur at any stage (`notSent`, `maybeDelivered`, `responseReceived`) and carries the caller's or `shutdown`'s `Std.CancellationReason`.
   - **Prior art**: Go (`context.Canceled` / `context.DeadlineExceeded` vs `http.ErrServerClosed` / transport idle close), Rust `reqwest`/`hyper` (`is_timeout()` / cancelled vs pool closed), and .NET `HttpClient` (`TaskCanceledException` with `CancellationToken`) all distinguish caller cancellation from pool closure.

2. **Making cancellation an exchange-core event (`ExchangeEvent.cancel`)**:
   - **Verdict**: Sound choice. Routing cancellation through `Exchange.step` via `failOrDrop (.cancelled reason)` reuses the core's single source of truth for `delivery`, `written`, `status`, and `.draining` termination (`finishNow false`), and ensures existing invariants (`Observed`, `Timers`, non-reusability) automatically cover it.

3. **Reporting a cancellation before a request goes out to the planner as `Outcome.failure` with `notSent`**:
   - **Verdict**: Good pragmatic choice. Passing `notSent (.cancelled reason)` to `Call.plan` before the first request, after a backoff sleep, or before following a redirect avoids adding a third constructor to `Call.Outcome` (which would touch every theorem in `LeanHttp/Call/Proofs.lean`) while keeping every call termination recorded in `Call.Event.decide` and checked by `Call.validate`.

4. **`Client.close` graceful plus `Client.shutdown` cancelling in-flight calls**:
   - **Verdict**: Good separation of concerns, matching OkHttp (`ConnectionPool.evictAll()` vs `Dispatcher.cancelAll()`) and Go (`Transport.CloseIdleConnections()` vs cancelling a root `context.Context`), *provided* Defects 1 and 2 above are fixed so `Client.shutdown` actually reports `Cause.cancelled .shutdown` rather than racing with `Client.close`'s `Cause.localClose`.

5. **Looking names up once per call (`Progress.resolved`)**:
   - **Verdict**: The right trade-off for Lean v4.34.1. Because `uv_getaddrinfo` cannot be cancelled in v4.34.1 and an abandoned lookup occupies a libuv worker thread and can delay or crash process exit, avoiding repeated lookups across connect retries and redirects within a single call (whose lifetime is typically seconds, well within DNS TTLs) directly reduces exposure to abandoned lookups.
   - **Prior art**: Matches OkHttp's `RouteSelector` (which resolves once per call/exchange route enumeration and steps through the resolved addresses across retries) and curl's per-handle DNS cache, as opposed to Go's `net.Dialer` (which resolves per dial, acceptable in Go because `net.Resolver` can cancel or detach lookups without crashing at exit).

What the change sets out to do:

1. Cancelling a call.  Calls (Client.request, upload, send, withResponse, withUpload in LeanHttp/Client.lean) take an optional Std.CancellationToken.  Cancelling it should behave like the call's deadline firing early: a cause of its own (Cause.cancelled), the same delivery reporting (Error.delivery: notSent / maybeDelivered / responseReceived), never retried by the planner, a pending receive cancelled, and what Lean v4.34.1 cannot cancel (connect, write, name lookup, a running producer of a streamed request body) abandoned exactly as the deadline abandons it.  Cancelling while in the response-body callback, during a streamed upload, during the TLS handshake and during a retry's back-off sleep must have defined behaviour.  A cancelled call must never leave a connection in the pool that has a partly written request or an unread response on it.  Client.close stays graceful; a new Client.shutdown cancels calls in flight.
2. Name resolution: a call now looks each host name up once and reuses the addresses for every later round of connect attempts within the call (retries, redirects to the same host, fresh connections).

The architecture: pure step-function cores (the exchange core LeanHttp/Exchange.lean with theorems in LeanHttp/Exchange/Proofs.lean, Liveness.lean, Response.lean; the per-call planner LeanHttp/Call.lean with LeanHttp/Call/Proofs.lean; the record check LeanHttp/Call/Trace.lean) and a thin Async shell (LeanHttp/Client.lean, LeanHttp/Body.lean, LeanHttp/Connect.lean, LeanHttp/Transport.lean, and the new LeanHttp/Cancel.lean).  The toolchain's Std sources (Std.Sync.CancellationToken, Std.Async.Select) are not in the tree; their relevant behaviour: CancellationToken.cancel resolves each registered consumer (a Selectable.one waiter is raced with Waiter.race); its selector's unregisterFn removes consumers whose waiter has finished; Selectable.one registers every selector, awaits the waiter's promise, and calls every unregisterFn in a finally; Selectable.combine delivers a winner through IO.bindTask (not inline).  IO.Promise.resolve: only the first call has an effect.

Third round: both reviewers found no defect in the code.  Acted on: the controls now run the unit checks against the planted "checks ask only the signal" defect; the planted "abandon drains" defect is run against cancel-steps too; cancelon now works with trace=0; race's docstring rewrapped.  Gemini's one finding (a receive left pending when Live.next replaces a completion) it refuted itself in the same report: the cancel makes the exchange terminal in that step, and perform cancels the receive.

Since then, prompted by a benchmark (reused GETs over loopback, client pinned to one CPU, randomised blocks against main 27a0d86): the first version made a call 27% slower without a token, because Selectable.one hands its win and the end of the call on through thread-pool tasks, and every call watched the client's own token.  Changes (fixes.diff, since 5612056):
- The client keeps a table of its calls in flight (Client.inflight); shutdownNow cancels the client's token, then fires each call in the table.  Only a caller's token is watched per call.
- A caller's token is watched by registering a waiter with its Selector directly (Waiter.mk is private to Std.Async.Select; reached with `import all`), finished and unregistered at the end of the call; no Selectable.one.
- Cancel no longer has a signal promise: a state ref (unfired / fired with reason / ended), a bell ref that is none until a wait arms one, and hooks (Array (BaseIO Unit)) instead of continuations chained on the signal.  A call given no token makes no promise.
- Because a token resolves its waiters inside cancel, holding its mutex and before it records the reason, firing asks the token nothing: it rings the bell and runs the hooks, without a reason; Live.next and Cancel.race ask reason? afterwards from their own tasks.  The mailbox's cancellation completion carries no reason.
Residual cost measured: about 6-9% slower without a token, about 14% with one (ablations of the table, the token check, Live.next's check and the hook each remove a point or two; a layout control, main with an unused function, is within 1-3%).

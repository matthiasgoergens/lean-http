What the author concluded about the first round's findings, and what changed (the changes are in fixes.diff, the whole change against main in review.diff):

- Confirmed and fixed: Selectable.one hands a cancellation on from a worker (IO.bindTask), so the call's signal lags token.cancel.  Client.shutdown (now renamed Client.shutdownNow) cancelled then closed; the close woke a call waiting at the active cap, which found the pool closed and reported localClose.  Cancel.reason? now asks the tokens directly; the shell checks it at the top of every round of Attempt.acquire, before the pool-off closed check in Attempt.run, at the top of Attempt.connect (no lookup is started for a cancelled call), and Connect.round checks it once more after its loop (a round an attempt won as the call was cancelled now returns cancelled and drops the socket).  New tests: shutdown-cap, shutdown-backoff.
- Refuted: "a token cancelled before the call starts is not seen before watch returns".  Selectable.one calls the combined selector's tryFn first, and Selectable.combine's tryFn runs the case's continuation inline when a selector's tryFn returns some; CancellationToken.selector's tryFn returns some for a cancelled token.  So the signal is resolved inside watch.  (The cancel-before scenario, which checks that no connection is opened, passes in every run.)  Calls after a shutdown therefore were not affected; calls waiting at the cap were.
- Confirmed and fixed: per-wait continuations on the call's signal.  Each Cancel.race now arms a bell of its own (an IO.Promise in an IO.Ref, replaced by the next wait); one continuation per call on the signal rings the current bell.
- Confirmed and fixed: BodyStream.read handed out bytes the stream held after a cancellation.  It now steps the cancellation first when the exchange is live; new test cancel-held.
- Confirmed and fixed (docs): the withResponse docstring's "a body that had arrived in full is unaffected"; onCancel's "in the thread that cancels it"; "before k is entered".  Now: until the final head arrives the call fails; from then on reads raise; once the exchange has ended cancelling changes nothing.
- Fixed (statements): step_cancel now says which end each phase gets (draining: finish not reusable; otherwise: the failure), and step_cancel_not_reusable's docstring now says it is one step.
- Fixed (tests): checker property compares the reason and rejects a failure while draining; cancel-write checks the pool; cancel-redirect (cancellation at a redirect's second hop; the error's delivery is response received, because the redirect was a response); cancel-return now makes a second, uncancelled call that gets a fresh connection (cancelcalls=1); the calls after a shutdown or close are now checked against the planner; the record check's unit test of a non-cancellation failure in place of a request now fails only on that rule.
- Accepted with a rename: Client.shutdown -> Client.shutdownNow (Java's ExecutorService/HttpClient naming).
- Accepted as documented limits rather than code changes (NOTES.md "Left open"): a producer started inline holds off cancellation until its first wait (as it holds off the deadline; pre-existing); the cost of unregistering from a token shared by many calls; a cancelled or timed-out cap wait leaves its continuation on the client's freed promise until a connection comes back (as before); each exchange's mailbox lives until the call ends; a cancellation between the head and the callback is seen by the first read.
- Not changed: a connection the call took and wrote nothing on goes back to the pool (a fresh or idle connection with nothing written is clean); the docs now say so instead of "closed, never pooled".  The acquire timeout path re-enters acquire, as it did before the change (awaitFirst's timeout also matched `some _`); now written as an explicit match.
- Lookups: the docstrings now say a host is looked up again only after a lookup raised an exception.
- Not tested by sockets (judged impractical to time): a cancellation exactly between two hops, and one during a drain; the core's traces and the record check cover those paths.

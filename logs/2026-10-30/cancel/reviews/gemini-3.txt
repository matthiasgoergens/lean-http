### 2. Design Decisions

- **A separate `Cause.cancelled` vs. reusing `Cause.localClose`:** Separate `Cause.cancelled (reason : Std.CancellationReason)` is the right choice. Callers need to distinguish pool/client closure (`Client.close`) from per-call or shutdown cancellation and inspect the `Std.CancellationReason` (`.cancel`, `.deadline`, `.shutdown`, `.custom`). Prior art (Go `context.Canceled` vs. `http.ErrServerClosed`/transport errors, Rust `hyper`/Java `HttpClient`) likewise separates caller cancellation from pool lifecycle errors.
- **`ExchangeEvent.cancel` in the exchange core:** Making cancellation a pure core event keeps delivery accounting, byte counts, and connection reuse decisions (`step_cancel`, `step_cancel_not_reusable`) inside the verified state machine rather than duplicating phase-dependent logic in the shell. Skipping the body drain on `.cancel` is also right: cancellation requests immediate termination rather than spending up to `drainMs` reading unread bytes to save a socket.
- **Reporting pre-send cancellation to the planner as a `.notSent` failure:** Reporting `notSent (.cancelled reason)` to `Call.plan` before a hop is sent (initial request, after redirect drain, or after retry sleep) keeps `Call.validate` and the trace model uniform without adding a special `Outcome` constructor that every planner theorem would have to case-split on.
- **Graceful `Client.close` + `Client.shutdownNow`:** Preserving graceful `Client.close` while adding `Client.shutdownNow` (matching Java's `ExecutorService.shutdownNow` and separating idle eviction from active-call abort as in Go's `CloseIdleConnections` + `context.CancelFunc` and OkHttp's `evictAll` + `cancelAll`) is clean and unambiguous.
- **Per-call DNS caching (`Progress.resolved`):** Given that `uv_getaddrinfo` cannot be cancelled in Lean v4.34.1 and an abandoned lookup holds a libuv worker thread (and can segfault during runtime exit), resolving each hostname at most once per call—matching OkHttp's `RouteSelector`—minimizes exposure to uncancelable lookups across retries, redirects, and stale-connection re-dispatches without introducing a process-wide DNS cache without TTLs.

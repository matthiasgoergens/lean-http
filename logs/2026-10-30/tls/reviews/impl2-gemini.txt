To get past that block, [`/close-after`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-wt-tls/tests/net/tls.py#L173-L174) starts `threading.Timer(0.05, uncork)` *before* calling `notify(conn, n)`.

The HTTP response then leaves in its own TCP segment without `close_notify`; Call 0 sees `session.clean == true` and pools `conn 0`; and with `idlecheck=off` ([tests/net/run.sh:1111](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-wt-tls/tests/net/run.sh#L1111)), Call 1 reuses `conn 0`, hits the late `close_notify`, and sets `redispatched=true`, failing line 1113.
  2. **Why `tls-latched` passes without testing `s->latched`**:
     [`/close-after`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-wt-tls/tests/net/tls.py#L165) sends `response("200 OK", b"bye\n")`, which carries `Content-Length: 4`. When the response and `close_notify` arrive in one read, [`lean_http_tls_read`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-wt-tls/native/tls.c#L510-L524) decrypts the 43-byte response on the first `SSL_read` and consumes the `close_notify` record on the second `SSL_read` (which sets `SSL_RECEIVED_SHUTDOWN` inside OpenSSL and `s->latched = RD_CLOSED`).

     - In [`lean_http_tls_clean`](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-wt-tls/native/tls.c#L545-L549), `!(SSL_get_shutdown(s->ssl) & LH_SSL_RECEIVED_SHUTDOWN)` is already `false`, so `s->latched == 0` at line 545 is redundant with `LH_SSL_RECEIVED_SHUTDOWN`: deleting `if (total > 0) s->latched = (int)end;` ([native/tls.c:520](file:///home/matthias/prog/lean-http-wt-tls/native/tls.c#L520)) leaves `tls-latched` passing.
